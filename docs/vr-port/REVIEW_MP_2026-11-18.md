**Method**: three areas (voice, the datagram layer, the stores and QuakeC) were first read by delegated reviewers;
every finding below that came from them was then checked against the code here, and the ones fixed were rebuilt.
Two were run: the header-length crash (F-fix 2) was reproduced against the unfixed binary with a 13-line script (a
connect request, then 8 bytes: the dedicated server died with SIGSEGV) and does not happen with the fix; a dedicated
server starts and runs e1m1 with all fixes. Nothing else could be exercised: there is no headless client here, so no
fix below was run against a real second player and the suite (`mp_test.py`) was not run.

**Counts**: 7 fixes committed (2 remote crashes or hangs of the server, 1 leak, 3 voice defects, 1 unchecked client
value); 17 findings written up (1 high, 8 medium, 8 low).

**Overall**: the architecture is sound and conventional where it matters: one authority, QuakeWorld-style prediction
with a time credit against speed hacks, acknowledged delta snapshots with a priority accumulator under a rate, moves
sent as quantized deltas with repeats and idempotent by number, lag compensation scoped and capped. Parsers of client
data are careful (lengths, counts and slots checked nearly everywhere; the speaker and the sender slot always come
from the connection). The weak side is the same in several places: **values the owning client is trusted for are not
yet bounded** (finding 1: MP_DESIGN's P2-6 is not done), and **the datagram layer still has NetQuake's lack of any
per-connection secret** while the branch exposes servers to the internet through room codes (finding 2).

## 1. The owner's values are taken as sent: no caps on hands, room-scale motion, fists or throw speeds

- **Category**: architecture / correctness (trust). **Severity**: high for deathmatch and public room codes, low for
  coop among friends.
- **Where**: `Quake/vr/vr_server.cpp:1662-1790` (`applyVrMove`), `Quake/vr/vr_netmove.cpp:52-155` (`visitMove`: the
  move's own origin and everything relative to it), `Quake/vr/vr_pmove.cpp:1591-1604` (the room-scale walk),
  `Quake/vr/vr_clientprefs.cpp` `rig_f` / `rigFrame_f`, `Quake/vr/vr_heldpose.cpp:378-390` (`grabSpot_f`).
- **Finding**: MP_PLAN 2.2 item 2 says the owner's body is the owner's and "the server validates (caps on hand
  distance, throw speed, hotspot reach)"; MP_DESIGN lists it as P2-6. None of it exists yet. A move's hand, muzzle,
  loading-port, magazine and lamp places are `origin + offset` with both sent by the client; `rebaseHands` moves them
  onto the server's origin, so the offset is what counts, and it is an int32 of 64ths: up to 2^25 units. They are
  written straight into the edict's fields that QC shoots, swings, grabs and reloads from. Concretely a modified
  client can:
  - put its muzzle (`muzzlePos`, `shotRot`) and its hands anywhere on the map: shoot from behind a wall or from
    inside another player, punch, grab weapons and disarm players across the level;
  - send `roomscaleMove = (100000, 0, 0)`: `playerMoveOne` sets the velocity to it for a second `walkMove` each move
    (collision still applies, the time credit only caps `dt`), a speed hack of any size in open space;
  - send `throwVel`, `vel`, `velMag` of any size (melee damage and throws scale with them);
  - send a fist of 128 spheres of 500 units radius (`rig_f` accepts shorts / 64 with no bound): the server's grab test
    and its Box3D hand body then reach, and shove, everything in a 500-unit ball;
  - claim any hotspot of any entity for its empty hand (`vr_grabspot`), and always the full lag-compensation rewind
    (`viewBack`, capped by `vr_net_lagcomp_most` only).
- **Recommended fix**: one validation step at the top of `applyVrMove` (it is the single funnel for clc_move, the
  QVR moves and the buffered ones): clamp each hand, muzzle, port and head offset from the move's origin to an arm's
  reach plus the held weapon's length (a constant such as 2.5 m scaled by world scale is enough to start), the
  room-scale speed to a walking head's (about 3 m/s), the hand and throw speeds to a real arm's (about 30 m/s), and in
  `rig_f` each sphere to a hand's size. Count the clamps per client (`vr_mp_status`) so a real player hitting one is
  seen in testing. Note that a clamped value disagrees with the client's prediction (the hang and mantle moves read
  the hands): clamp in `inputOf` identically, or accept a correction for a cheater.
- **Drawbacks**: bounds must be generous enough for long two-handed weapons, tall players and tracking glitches, or
  honest players see their hands snap; each bound is a tuning constant to maintain; it does not stop aim assistance
  or wall-seeing, only the gross abuses.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in 0667623f2 (vr_moveguard.hpp/.cpp: places within 3 m of the move's origin at a world scale of at least 1.5, speeds 40 m/s, spin 150 rad/s, fist spheres 0.3 m / 0.1 m, grab spots within reach, the view's delay 0.55 s and the move's time twice the round trip plus 0.5 s; prediction bounds identically in inputOf; counted in vr_mp_status `mp_guard:`). Not as suggested: the room-scale walk is a budget (6 m/s saved up to 2 m), not a 3 m/s speed cap, which an honest lean's recentring or a snap turn's body swing (up to ~2000 u/s for one frame) would have hit; the drawn hands, holsters and ammo pouch are not bounded (others' drawing; a frame behind at a spawn or teleport); a throw's release point is held but not counted (it trails the hand's history); at vr_net_lagcomp_most's default 0.5 s that cap stays the tighter rewind bound. Tests: vr_moveguard_selftest 17/0, new mp_test.py guard 12/0 (honest tall player with a rocket launcher: nothing held; a lying client: every kind held and counted); honest tests' mp_guard all 0 (prefs, rules, grab, held, disarm, pickup, forcegrab, moves, avatarcal, guests, dm, coop, cosmetics, lagcomp, lockstep_throw 30/0; melee, blade, predict, predictclimb with their pre-existing failures only).

## 2. No per-connection secret; a spoofed connect request kicks a player whose address the server publishes

- **Category**: architecture / correctness. **Severity**: medium (high for a server reachable from the internet).
- **Where**: `Quake/net_dgrm.c:1590` (`CCREP_PLAYER_INFO` writes `client->netconnection->address`, the player's
  `ip:port`), `:1723-1746` (a connect request from an address and port already connected, older than 2 s, closes that
  connection), `:909-945` (the unreliable sequence compare), the held-chunk slots in `TakeChunk`.
- **Finding**: this is NetQuake's design, but the branch routes every client through one socket, adds room codes
  that invite strangers, and rewrote the affected code. Anyone can ask a server for each player's `ip:port`
  (`CCREQ_PLAYER_INFO`, unauthenticated), then send one spoofed 20-byte `CCREQ_CONNECT` from that address: the server
  runs `NET_Close(s)` on the live player. B25 added a silence test for the same-name, other-port case but not for
  this one. The same spoofed source can freeze a player (one unreliable datagram with sequence `0xFFFFFFFE` makes
  every later real one "stale": the compare is unsigned with no window) or inject a reliable chunk up to 15 ahead
  (sequences start at 0; the forged chunk is held and the real one ignored: an `svc_stufftext` to a client).
- **Recommended fix**: (a) small: in the `ret == 0` branch close the old connection only when it has been silent
  for `QVR_GHOST_SILENCE`, as the branch above it does, and write the address without its port in
  `CCREP_PLAYER_INFO` (or not at all to non-local askers); (b) the real one: a random 32-bit value from each side in
  the handshake (the client's in `CCREQ_CONNECT`, the server's in `CCREP_ACCEPT`) used as the initial sequence
  numbers of both channels, and a bounded forward window on the unreliable compare. No header bytes are added.
- **Drawbacks**: (a) delays a genuine reconnect from the same port by up to 3 s (the client's retries cover it, but
  this is untested here, which is why it was not committed); the server browser or `status` output may use the port.
  (b) is a protocol change (`QVR_NET_VERSION` bump) and touches the harness's proxy if it rewrites sequences.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `37d58dd84` (both parts; QVR_NET_VERSION 39; the unreliable window 1024 ahead, 256 behind, resynced after 3 s of nothing in it; the port given only to an asker at 127.0.0.1); tests: new `mp_test.py spoof` 20 passed, 0 failed (before: 7 passed, 2 failed); retransmit, rejoin, version, vanish, fuzz, nat, coop all passed; jitter's lift check flaky under load, before and after alike.

## 3. The listen host can be given a slot other than 0 when loading a coop save; "own = slot 0" is assumed everywhere

- **Category**: correctness / architecture. **Severity**: medium.
- **Where**: `Quake/vr/vr_coopsave.cpp:306-341` (`VR_CoopPickSlot`), `Quake/sv_main.c:638`; against
  `Quake/vr/vr_clientprefs.cpp:643` (`ownSlot()` returns 0 whenever hosting) and `Quake/vr/vr_progs.hpp:269`
  (`localPlayer()` is `svs.clients[0].edict`).
- **Finding**: in a loaded coop game a connecting player takes the pending slot saved under his name, else the first
  free slot nobody is pending for. The host's own loopback client goes through the same choice with `cl_name`. If the
  host changed his name since the save, or loads a save made by another host or a dedicated server, slot 0 is
  pending for someone else and the host lands in slot 1 or 2. `localPlayer()` is then null until the saved slot-0
  player joins, and after that it is the remote player: `publishOwn` writes the host's fists and palm frames into
  his slot, and every "own player" path (held pose, prediction's own source, the HUD's stats) acts on his edict.
- **Recommended fix**: the loopback connection always takes slot 0 (pass "is local" to `VR_CoopPickSlot`; when slot
  0 is pending for another name, swap the two saved slots' blocks before the pick). Longer term derive `ownSlot()` /
  `localPlayer()` from the loopback client's real slot instead of the constant.
- **Drawbacks**: the swap changes which saved character the renamed host gets (he takes slot 0's entity number and
  colours); deriving the slot touches every caller of `ownSlot()` and `localPlayer()` (not counted here) and the "nobody in slot 0 yet" cases.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in e5a305250; the saved blocks (name, level-start parms) are swapped, not the saved
  edicts (the host plays the body saved in slot 0, as the drawbacks say); `ownSlot()`/`localPlayer()` from the
  loopback's real slot (`progs::hostSlot`), a dedicated server's `localPlayer()` unchanged; tests: campaign_listen's new
  host-slot part (renamed Z, renamed B) 15 passed 3 failed before, 18/0 after; campaign 18/0, rejoin 18/0, coop 12/0.

## 4. Voice relay: the listener's budget goes to speakers in slot order, redundant copies included

- **Category**: correctness / architecture. **Severity**: medium.
- **Where**: `Quake/vr/vr_voicerelay.cpp:152-231` (`serverWrite`).
- **Finding**: for each speaker in slot order the loop writes his fresh frames and then his repeat copies out of the
  listener's bucket (`vr_net_voice_bytes`, 8000 B/s) and the datagram's quarter before the next speaker gets
  anything. One talker costs about 3400 B/s fresh plus 3400 B/s of copies. With two talking at once the lower slot
  takes about 6800 and the higher gets about a third of his frames (the rest age out after 0.3 s); a third talker is
  silent. Mute is client-side only, so a slot-0 player with an open microphone starves everyone else's voice even
  for listeners who muted him, and a listener with voice chat off is still sent everything.
- **Recommended fix**: two passes: fresh frames for every speaker first, starting at a slot that rotates per
  datagram; copies with what remains. Then let a client tell the server its mute mask and voice-off (one byte in the
  prefs) so the relay skips those speakers for him.
- **Drawbacks**: under a tight budget nobody gets redundancy (loss is heard sooner, though Opus's in-band FEC still
  covers single losses); the mute mask is one more per-player store to reset.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `4f072ffbe` (the harness's lossy bounds in `24f1438b8`); the mask travels as a client
  command, `vr_voicechat_listen <mask> <off>` (reliable, on each change and at each signon; whitelisted in `sv_user.c`),
  not a byte in the prefs (another group's file); a muted speaker who talks is noted to the listener (a `QVR_SVC_VOICE`
  of no frames, at most every 0.1 s) so the Voice Chat page's "muted, talking" stays; no `QVR_NET_VERSION` change.
  Tests: `vr_voicechat_selftest` relay cases (two talkers whole, three 0.80-0.81 each, the rotation, mute and voice off
  skipped); `mp_test.py voice` 27/0 (the server sends a muted speaker's frames no more; A and C both heard), `--listen`
  26/0, `--lag 50 --jitter 30 --loss 0.05` 28/0.

## 5. A zero-length UDP datagram ends each read of the shared server socket

- **Category**: correctness (denial of service). **Severity**: medium.
- **Where**: `Quake/net_dgrm.c:423` (`while ((length = driver->Read (...)) > 0)`), `Quake/net_udp.c:277-290`,
  `Quake/net_wins.c` `WINS_Read`.
- **Finding**: `recvfrom` returns 0 for an empty datagram, the same value `UDP_Read` returns for "nothing waiting"
  (the comment at `net_udp.c:271` notes it). Since B23 every client is read from this one socket, so each empty
  datagram stops the drain for all of them until the next call. A stream of empty datagrams to port 26000, from
  anyone, with no handshake, at a rate above the number of `ReadShared` calls a second makes real packets queue
  behind them in the socket buffer and then drop, for every player at once.
- **Recommended fix**: make the two cases distinguishable: have the read functions return a distinct value (for
  example -2) for `EWOULDBLOCK`, or loop inside `UDP_Read`/`WINS_Read` while `recvfrom` returns 0 (bounded to a few
  hundred a call), and keep the `> 0` test for real data.
- **Drawbacks**: touches both platform drivers and every caller that tests the return value (`Datagram_GetMessage`'s
  client side, the test and search polls); Windows was not buildable here.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `64704934a` (the drivers' Read skips up to 256 empty datagrams a call, and so does the -natgateway shim; callers unchanged); tests: new `mp_test.py emptyflood` (20000 a second) 14 passed, 0 failed (before: 6 passed, 1 failed); coop, browser, nat passed. Windows' WINS_Read not built.

## 6. Blocking DNS lookups on the game thread (room service, server browser)

- **Category**: performance. **Severity**: medium for a VR listen host (a frozen frame in a headset).
- **Where**: `Quake/net_dgrm.c` `RoomResolve` (called from `RoomHostTick`, `gethostbyname`), and the server
  browser's search send (one lookup per favourite or recent server, up to 24).
- **Finding**: `vr_mp_room 1` and `vr_mp_rendezvous vittorioromeo.com:26100` are the defaults, so every multiplayer
  host resolves the name at start and again every 60 s while it fails. With no working DNS (a LAN party, a captive
  portal, a headset on a hotspot) each attempt blocks for the resolver's timeout, several seconds, inside
  `Host_Frame`: the host's view freezes and every client stalls. A dead favourite hostname does the same at each
  browser refresh. (Observed side effect while testing: a dedicated server started here registered with the room
  service and printed its room code and public address: expected with the default, but worth knowing for test runs.)
- **Recommended fix**: resolve on a worker (`jobs::async`, as the microphone's open does) and take the result on a
  later frame; back off exponentially on failure; never start a lookup while remote clients are connected.
- **Drawbacks**: `gethostbyname` is not re-entrant on every platform (use `getaddrinfo`); one more future to drain at
  shutdown.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `0daeb5dd2` (`vr_netlookup.cpp`: getaddrinfo on `jobs::async`, back-off 2 s doubling to 5 min, none started while remote clients are connected, waited for at shutdown; a room-code join waits at most 3 s); tests: new `mp_test.py lookup` 9 passed, 0 failed; browser, netsafe, nat passed.

## 8. Rejoin keeps a leaver by unauthenticated name; the table never evicts; stale level-start parms

- **Category**: correctness / architecture. **Severity**: medium on a long-running dedicated coop server, low on a
  listen host.
- **Where**: `Quake/vr/vr_coopsave.cpp:345-362` (`VR_CoopNoteLeave`), `:306-318` (`VR_CoopPickSlot`), `:413-420`
  (`VR_CoopRejoinParms`).
- **Finding**: three related defects.
  1. `leavers[16]` entries are freed only when that name returns or the server shuts down; a level change keeps
     them. After 16 distinct names have left, `k < 0` and no later leaver is recorded: the feature silently stops.
     One visitor joining and leaving under 16 names does it.
  2. Anyone connecting under a leaver's name takes his slot and inventory. Two players with the default name
     collide: the second leaver overwrites the first's record.
  3. `startParms` is copied at leave time and never refreshed at a level change, so a player who left on e1m1 and
     returns on e1m2 spawns with what he carried (correct) but respawns after a death with e1m1's start gear.
  Also `!sv.loadgame` disables the leaver path for the whole of a level loaded from a coop save, which is the main
  way a coop game is continued: a saved player who crashes and returns during that level is a new player.
- **Recommended fix**: stamp entries with `realtime` and overwrite the oldest when full; key the record by name plus
  the connect request's `vr_mp_clientkey` (already carried and stored as `qvrKey`); refresh `start*` from `now*` for
  every used leaver where `SV_SaveSpawnparms` runs; gate the leaver path on "this slot is not pending" instead of
  `!sv.loadgame`.
- **Drawbacks**: a key makes "rejoin from another PC" fail (acceptable: he is then a new player). The key is
  `vr_mp_clientkey`, archived, so it survives a crash once the config has been written with it.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in 8dbf7ca15; also a coop load that keeps the players forgets the leavers of the game
  left behind (no shutdown there); tests: new rejoin_keep (key, level change, 17 leavers, loaded level) 6 passed
  5 failed before, 11/0 after; rejoin 18/0, campaign 18/0, campaign_listen 18/0, flow 16/1 (Load Autosave's list:
  the same failure before the change).

## 9. Harness commands are accepted from any client in every build

- **Category**: correctness (trust) / simplification. **Severity**: medium-low.
- **Where**: `Quake/sv_user.c:675-678` (the whitelist), `Quake/vr/vr_lagcomp.cpp:504-581` (`punch_f`, `shot_f`),
  `QC/weapons.qc:4750-4767` (impulses 190-195, outside `QVR_TESTS`).
- **Finding**: `vr_lagcomp_shot` and `vr_lagcomp_punch` exist for the harness ("a test shot, judged and reported,
  nothing hurt") but any client may send them. Each runs two `SV_Move` traces with hit models (or two hit-model
  sweeps), a lag-compensation rewind of every target up to 1 s back (not the cvar's cap), and a `Con_Printf` on the
  server. A client can pack about a dozen into every packet: thousands of rewinds and console lines a second, into
  the host's console and its log file without bound. The coordinates are parsed with `Q_atof` with no finite or range
  check before they reach `SV_Move`. Impulses 190-195 give any coop player a loaded shotgun in a holster.
- **Recommended fix**: accept the two commands only when a harness cvar is set on the server (`vr_mp_watch` or
  `developer`), and move the impulses under `QVR_TESTS`. More generally give client string commands a per-client
  budget, as `VR_GuestMayTalk` does for talk: `vr_avatarcal` is relayed reliably to every client on each change with
  no limit (MP_PLAN B29 already lists name and colour floods; the same guard covers this).
- **Drawbacks**: the harness must set the cvar on its servers; a budget needs a number that the legitimate bursts
  (the fist's 6 `vr_rig` commands at spawn) fit under.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in 7995b51ea (vr_lagcomp_shot/_punch only with the server's vr_mp_watch, which the harness sets on every game, points finite; impulses 190-195 under QVR_TESTS; VR_ClientCommandBudget: 64 commands at once, 32 a second per remote client, joining commands and the host's own exempt, counted in vr_mp_status `mp_cmds:`). Tests: new mp_test.py cmdguard 11/0 (refused without vr_mp_watch, taken with it; both players' spawn commands 16 with 57+ left; a 150-command flood: 64 run, 86 dropped); lagcomp 15/0, cosmetics 23/0, disarm 38/0, guests 13/0, avatarcal 10/0; release QC (-DQVR_NO_TESTS) 0 warnings.

## 10. Voice play-out replays 2-second-old audio when a frame takes longer than the 40 ms lead

- **Category**: correctness. **Severity**: medium for flat-screen or struggling clients, low at 72 Hz.
- **Where**: `Quake/vr/vr_voicechat.cpp:58` (`lead = 0.04`), `pump` and `emit`.
- **Finding**: each speaker is a 2 s looping in-memory sound; `pump` decodes only up to the channel's read position
  plus 40 ms. The mixer paints up to `_snd_mixahead` (0.1 s) between two pumps, and the ring is never cleared behind
  the reader, so whatever lies past the write position is what was written 2 s earlier. The underrun branch runs
  only after that audio has already been mixed. At 20 frames a second a listener hears about 10 ms of 2-second-old
  speech in every 50 ms of anyone who has talked longer than 2 s; any hitch gives a burst. (The mixer's read of a
  looping sound was not traced line by line.)
- **Recommended fix**: in `pump`, zero the span of the ring between the previous read position and the current one
  (wrap-aware) so an over-read plays silence; or make `lead` at least `_snd_mixahead`.
- **Drawbacks**: the first costs a memset per speaker per frame; the second adds 60 ms of latency to every voice.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `b8972b3cd` (the first option: the ring zeroed from the last pump's read position to
  this one's, wrap-aware); tests: `vr_voicechat_selftest` (a simulated ring over 2.5 laps: 0 of 2160 samples read past
  the written end stale, 830 without the zeroing). Not run against the real mixer (the harness is `-nosound`).

## 11. Room service: spoofed registrations fill the table; refusals are not authenticated

- **Category**: architecture. **Severity**: medium for the service, low for the engine.
- **Where**: `Misc/rendezvous/qvr_rendezvous.py` (`Service.register`, `Service.allow`, `Service.join`),
  `Quake/net_dgrm.c:302-329`.
- **Finding**: the token bucket and the rooms-per-address cap are keyed by the datagram's claimed source with no
  return-routability check: 20000 spoofed 64-byte `REGISTER`s from distinct addresses fill `max_rooms` for 120 s, and
  repeating it every two minutes refuses every new host. A `JOIN` with a spoofed source and a valid code makes the
  host send 80 punch datagrams (2560 bytes) to the victim: bounded (8 peers a host, 20 joins per 10 s per room) but
  a 40-times reflector. In the engine `ROOM_REFUSED` and `ROOM_BADVERSION` are acted on without the nonce check the
  other replies get: a packet spoofed from the service's address stops registration for 600 s and the shared code
  dies after 120 s. A host whose router's port changes gets a new code although the comment promises his old one
  (the old room still exists, so `wanted not in self.rooms` is false).
- **Recommended fix**: a cookie round for `REGISTER` (reply with an HMAC of the source; create the room when it is
  echoed); check the nonce on `REFUSED` (the service already echoes it); in `register`, when the wanted code's room
  has the same source IP, move it to the new port.
- **Drawbacks**: the cookie is a service protocol change (version 2) and one more round trip before a host has his
  code.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `b3c6066ba` (room service protocol 2: COOKIE 0x87, an HMAC-SHA256 of the address and the period; the nonce checked on every reply the host acts on and a joiner's BADVERSION, which now echoes it; a room moves to its host's new port from the same IP); tests: `test_rendezvous.py` 33 passed, 0 failed (13 new); new `mp_test.py roomnonce` 7 passed, 0 failed (before: 2 passed, 3 failed); nat, netsafe passed. The deployed service must be updated with the release.

## 12. `vr_net_rate` is the client's alone and the reliable resend has no real back-off

- **Category**: performance / architecture. **Severity**: low.
- **Where**: `Quake/vr/vr_netsnap.cpp:1224`, `Quake/vr/vr_voicerelay.cpp:141`; `Quake/net_dgrm.c:676-685`
  (`ResendTimeout`).
- **Finding**: the server has no ceiling of its own on what it sends a client: a client's rate of 0 means no cap
  (1400 B x 72 Hz, about 100 KB/s each, 1.6 MB/s for 16). And `ResendTimeout` is capped at 1.0 s whatever
  `resendBackoff` says (and is 1.0 s while no round trip is measured), so a peer that is not reading (loading a map,
  gone without a disconnect) gets the whole unacked window, up to 16 x 1208 bytes, every second for
  `net_messagetimeout` (300 s).
- **Recommended fix**: a server cvar `vr_net_rate_max` (`min` with the client's; 0 from the client then means the
  server's); let the back-off double past 1 s up to a few seconds once two timed resends went unanswered.
- **Drawbacks**: a longer back-off delays recovery after a long stall by up to that long.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `e27ff43c3` (`vr_net_rate_max`, default 80000, 0 none: a client's 0 is the server's, more is held to it; the resend timeout 2 s then 4 s at most after two unanswered timed resends); tests: new `mp_test.py backoff` 12 passed, 0 failed (before: 9 passed, 3 failed); retransmit, coop passed.

## 15. Voice: a rename clears a mute; the play-out touches sound channels without `S_MixJoin`

- **Category**: correctness. **Severity**: low.
- **Where**: `Quake/vr/vr_voicechat.cpp:924-929` (`resetSpeaker` on a name change), `:688-703`, `:733`, `:876-882`.
- **Finding**: a speaker's mute and volume are reset when his scoreboard name changes, so an abusive player binds
  `name x` / `name y` and escapes every mute, and a newcomer with the default name inherits the previous one's. The
  play-out reads `ch->pos` and writes the sound's data and the channel's origin and volume without joining an
  asynchronous mix first; every other VR module that touches channels calls `S_MixJoin()`. It is safe today only by
  frame order (the pump runs before `S_MixKick`); a screen update outside `Host_Frame` (the loading plaque) can leave
  a mix posted at the next pump.
- **Recommended fix**: reset a speaker's settings when the slot empties or a new client connects into it (the server
  can send the event; the client sees the name go empty), not on a rename; call `S_MixJoin()` at the top of `pump()`
  for an active speaker and in `receive()` before the read position is taken.
- **Drawbacks**: a player who really changes identity in the same slot keeps his mute (intended); the join can wait
  for a mix in flight (microseconds in practice, as for the other callers).
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `489e490c3` (the client sees the slot's name go empty while in the game; leaving the
  server resets every slot's); tests: `vr_voicechat_selftest` (kept through a rename and a level change, reset when the
  slot empties), `mp_test.py voice` 27/0 (A renamed while muted is still muted at B and at the server). The
  `S_MixJoin` calls are not exercised by the harness (`-nosound`).

## 16. All voice DSP runs on the main thread, with a 0.5 s catch-up after a hitch

- **Category**: performance. **Severity**: low.
- **Where**: `Quake/vr/vr_voicechat.cpp:572-578` (`captureFrame`), `:711-720` (`emit`'s history ring).
- **Finding**: capture is polled with `SDL_DequeueAudio` and denoised and encoded on the render thread. After a
  hitch the backlog is drained in one frame, capped at half a second: 50 RNNoise blocks and 25 Opus encodes on top
  of the frame that was already late. Each speaker's 1 s float history (192 KB, a modulo per sample) is written
  every frame for `vr_voicechat_report` alone.
- **Recommended fix**: cap the catch-up at about 100 ms and drop the rest (old speech is worthless); write the
  history only while the report cvar is on. Moving capture to a worker is only worth it if profiles show it.
- **Drawbacks**: a hitch then loses up to 0.4 s of the talker's speech instead of delaying it.
- **Status (2026-11-19)**: done in `65885672c`; the history is written for 30 s after a `vr_voicechat_report` (a
  command, not a cvar: a cvar would have touched `vr_cvars.inc`); no worker. Tests: `vr_voicechat_selftest` (4800 of
  24000 samples waiting taken; no history without a report), `mp_test.py voice` 27/0.
