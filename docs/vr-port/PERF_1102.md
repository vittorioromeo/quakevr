# Performance check of 2026-11-02: armed humanoids (Tracy, CPU + QC + GPU)

His words (2026-11-01 23:40): "It feels like the performance has gotten worse. Probably it's the armed humanoids work".
Branch `agent/perf1102` (from `next` at `7b57439bf`), worker tree `nailburn`.

**Short answer: yes, there is a regression, and it is the armed humanoids.** With plain monsters the game costs what
1.1.0-beta4 did (`crowd32_plain` 2.13 against 2.11 ms CPU busy p50; `combat_48` and E1M1 with the replacements off within
0.1-0.2 ms). An armed humanoid costs about **0.16 ms of CPU a frame more than a plain monster** on `next` (0.06 in
beta4: +55% on the same 16-humanoid scene, `armed_16` 3.20 -> 4.94 ms). And since 2026-10-31 (`af93e6e3b`, "Promote
defaults: vr_agrunt_repl 1") every map's grunts and enforcers are armed humanoids, and his config arms the knights and
death knights too (`vr_agknight 1`, `vr_agdknight 1`): the ordinary fights got that cost everywhere (E1M1's fight 1.60 ->
4.99 ms, `combat_48` 3.09 -> 6.06). GPU: no regression (the eyes are cheaper than in beta4 in every comparable scene).

Three fixes (identical results, checked) take **0.9-1.25 ms p50 and 1.3-2.3 ms p95** off the armed fights; the rest
of the per-humanoid cost is listed below for him to decide (the main one is the arms' IK on the client, 30 us each a frame).

## Left, ranked by what it could save (for him)

1. **The arms' IK on the client**: `agarms::solve` 30 us per armed humanoid a frame (0.92 ms for 31), every frame, every
   one, seen or not. A quality trade would cut it (solve far or unseen ones every other frame, or not at all off screen):
   his call, not done.
2. **The QC's per-humanoid pose work**: `weapondrawnpose` ~30 calls per humanoid a frame (0.34 ms for 32) plus
   `VR_AGP_Pose`, `VR_AGK_*`, `VR_AG_WeaponLine` (63 calls a frame), `VR_AG_XReach`: about 1 ms for 32. Exact
   optimisations are possible (the pose keys looked up once a frame per humanoid), a larger QC refactor.
3. **The default `vr_agrunt_repl 1`** (2026-10-31): every grunt and enforcer of every map an armed one, about 0.1 ms
   each after the fixes (E1M1's fight 1.8 ms with plain grunts against 3.35 armed, Release). A setting, his decision.
4. Remaining `traceline` / `hitmodel_segment` cost through armed bodies (a per-humanoid triangle hierarchy for the
   skinned mesh would cut the triangle tests further).

### 1. The arms' IK level of detail (`vr_agarms_lod`, Graphics > Performance)

`agarms::lodReuse` (vr_agarms.cpp) stands in for `agarms::solve` on a far armed humanoid's frames that are not its
turn. Settings (all archived): `vr_agarms_lod 1` (0: every one every frame, as before), `vr_agarms_lod_near 192` (units
from the view: every frame within), `vr_agarms_lod_far 2` (in view: solved every 2nd frame), `vr_agarms_lod_unseen 6`
(out of view: every 6th), `vr_agarms_lod_margin 20` (degrees: "in view" is the eyes' widest corners, their cant, and
this round them, from the last drawn view; a turn of the head brings one in before it is drawn). Turns spread by the
entity's number.

- **Between solves** the last solve is carried by the chest as drawn now (the arms keep their joint turns on the body);
  a held weapon is carried by its target instead (its turn is the server's exactly: the shots' drawn line,
  `vr_agrunt_shot_drawn`), its hand with it, the off hand on its second grip or cupping with the right weapon's.
- **Always solved**: near ones; a hit reaction (`hitreact::reacting`); the Pose/Rig Studio's subject, the Live tape,
  the glitch finder, ik tags and debug; a change of the targets' shape (a grip taken or let go, a reach, a key's off
  hand); anything still easing (the jump ease, the second grip's ease, the off hand's goal change, the IK stretch, the
  arms easing in); arms that moved more than 0.3 units a frame (on the chest; a held hand on its target) between their
  last two solves. A reused frame keeps the arms "drawn this frame" (no ease-out; the ragdoll made from them takes them).
  The jump ease's acceleration is measured over the uneven time between solves (exactly as before when none was reused).
- **Hit tests (the choice)**: precise hits use the drawn mesh (PERF_1102 fix 1), so a reused frame's hits are the arms
  as drawn: what you see is what you hit. Melee, parries, tugs and grabs happen within reach, far inside 192 units, where
  every frame is solved: a skipped solve cannot change a melee or parry result. Shots at a far humanoid hit its arms as
  drawn (at most one frame behind the IK in view; the weapons' turn exact); disarm volumes the same.
- **Tools**: Debug > Tests > Check the Arms' Detail (`vr_agarms_lod_check 1`: each reused frame also solved on a copy
  and compared), Arms' Solve Time (`vr_agarms_cost`: solves, reused in and out of view, near, far on their turn, the
  far in-view hands' jerk, the check's distances). Tracy zone `armed pose: arms reused (lod)` beside `arms solve`.

Checks (fast mode, his settings): reused against the solve it stood for, `armed_mix32` (most humanoids at 200-600
units): hands 0.025 units mean, 5.4 most (74 of 9226 over 0.5: an off hand sliding on its second grip), held weapons'
turn 0.000 deg; E1M1's fight with the view turned 150 deg half way: 0.003 units mean, 0.64 most; `crowd32_armed` (near
160): 0.031 mean, 2.0 most. Looked at: three consecutive frames of `armed_mix32`'s far group (a reuse between two
solves): the weapons stay in the hands, nothing detaches or jumps.

Per armed humanoid: a reused frame costs a few transforms instead of the solve's 33 us; far ones in view cost half
their solves, out of view a sixth. `crowd32_armed` (fast mode, 600 frames): 25547 solves -> 16984 with near
160 (0.47 ms a frame less solving), 20285 with near 300. The bench scenes stand close (vrtesthall), so most of their
humanoids are "near"; real maps have more far ones.

### 2. The pose keys and the drawn weapon in one call each (exact)

New builtins (QC builtins.qc; vr_builtins_held.inc), their results in QC globals as traceline's in `trace_*`
(`progs::Bindings::agkPart`, `agDrawn`, `agExtent`, bound by name at progs load):

- `agposekey(code)`: VR_AGK_Add's key looked up once (`agpose::get`): `'1 0 0'` and its nine parts in `vr_agk_k_*`,
  instead of `weapondrawnpose(world, code, 18, 11..17, 25, 26)`: 10 calls and 10 table searches -> 1.
- `agdrawnframe(p)`: VR_AG_DrawnFrame's drawn weapon in `vr_ag_df_o`, `_fw`, `_up` (was 30, 31, 32: 3 calls, 3
  searches of the drawn list -> 1).
- `agweaponextent(p)`: VR_AG_WeaponLine's and VR_AG_MeleeLen's drawn extent in `vr_ag_wx_back`, `_front` (was 33, 34,
  35: 3 calls, each finding the hull's two farthest corners again -> 1).
QC keeps every decision and every sum (the same values through the same QC arithmetic); `weapondrawnpose` keeps its
codes (the tests use them). Not changed: `VR_AG_XReach` (its cost is its tracelines, the AI's), the Weapon Holds' 24
(a few calls a humanoid; a frame cache would be stale if a test changes a hold mid-frame).

Proof (a temporary build running the old calls beside the new, every call compared, then removed): pose keys 77824
humanoid-frames, drawn frames 196608, extents 192512 (`armed_mix32`, `armed_mix16_corpses`, 1500 frames each): 0
differences. agrunt tests 99 41/0, 79 36/0, 16 274/0.
